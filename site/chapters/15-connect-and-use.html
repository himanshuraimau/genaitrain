Chapter 15 · MCP, part 2 of 3

Connect one app. Then use it.

Step-by-step connections for Google Docs, Slides and Drive, Gmail and Calendar, Notion, Slack and Tableau. Then what to do when it breaks, and five everyday workflows.

Main tool in this chapter: Claude. Short notes for ChatGPT and Gemini are on page 8.

Before you connect anything

Three rules for your first connection

1

Read first

Your first prompts should only look at things: "list", "find", "summarize". Do not ask it to send, delete or edit until you trust the setup.

2

Test on something harmless

Use a practice page or a doc with nothing private in it. Not your payroll sheet.

3

Check the right account

If you have a work and a personal login, pick the one you mean on the sign-in page. Page 13 shows how to check afterwards.

A connector lets the AI act as you. Claude Help says connectors can read, create, change and delete data. Start small. Chapter 16 covers safety in full.

Walkthrough 1 · Claude

Google Docs, Slides and Drive

These are built-in connectors. You do not need a URL or a Google Cloud project.

  1. Open a chat in Claude (web or Desktop). Click the + button and hover over Connectors.
  2. Switch on Google Drive. Also switch on Google Docs, Google Sheets and Google Slides if you want Claude to edit them live.
  3. Sign in with your Google account when asked and allow access.
  4. Test: paste a Google Doc link and ask, "Summarize this document in 5 bullets."
What it can do
  • Search and read files in Drive, including Sheets, Slides, PDFs and Office files (text only).
  • Create new Docs, Sheets and Slides and edit them live in a pane beside the chat.
  • Read comments and suggestions in Docs.
Limits Claude Help states
  • Claude can't add charts to Google Slides.
  • Slides edits may not show live in the pane.
  • Live editing needs Chrome on the web, or Claude Desktop with its built-in browser on.
  • Team/Enterprise: an Owner must enable the connectors first.

Claude Help: Google Workspace connectors · official · Google's own Drive/Docs/Slides MCP servers are a separate developer-preview route (Chapter 14).

Walkthrough 2 · Claude

Gmail and Google Calendar

  1. In a chat: + → Connectors. Switch on Gmail and Google Calendar. Sign in to Google.
  2. Test with something read-only: "What is on my calendar tomorrow?"
  3. Then try: "Find the last email from [name] and summarize it."
  4. When you are ready, ask for a draft, not a send: "Draft a reply, don't send it."
What it can do
  • Gmail: search and read emails; draft, send, reply and forward.
  • Calendar: view events (including shared ones you can see); create, update, delete events; respond to invitations.
Built-in safety
  • By default Claude asks your approval before sending, replying, forwarding, sharing, moving or trashing.
  • It cannot read the contents of Gmail attachments.
  • On Team/Enterprise, owners decide whether people can skip the approval.

Habit: keep the approval prompts on. A wrongly sent email cannot be taken back.

Walkthrough 3 · Claude

Notion

Notion runs its own remote MCP server. You sign in with Notion (OAuth), and Claude can only see what you can see.

  1. Open Customize → Connectors and look for Notion in the directory. If it is there, click it and choose Connect.
  2. Not listed in your account? Click + Add → Add custom connector, name it "Notion" and paste https://mcp.notion.com/mcp. (This is the address used in Claude Code's own docs.)
  3. Sign in to Notion and approve access to your workspace.
  4. Test: "Search Notion for the page called [practice page] and summarize it."
What it can do

Search, read, create and update pages and databases, within what you can access.

Admin note

Workspace owners can manage which AI clients may connect (Settings → Notion MCP). Limiting clients for everyone needs the Enterprise plan. If you cannot connect, ask your Notion admin.

Walkthrough 4 · Claude

Slack

  1. Open Customize → Connectors, find Slack in the directory and click Connect.
  2. Sign in to your Slack workspace and review what it asks for.
  3. If Slack says an admin must approve it, ask your workspace admin. They can approve and manage all AI client connections.
  4. Test: "Search Slack for messages about [project name] from this week."
What it can do

Search Slack, send messages, work with canvases, upload files and use lists. Limits apply per tool, like Slack's normal API limits.

Why you use the directory here

Slack's docs say MCP clients must be backed by a registered Slack app (directory-published or internal), and that it does not support dynamic client registration. So pasting Slack's URL as a custom connector is not the route. Use the directory entry. our reading of Slack's docs

Sending messages is a write action. Keep your first tests to searching.

Walkthrough 5 · Claude

Tableau

Tableau has an official MCP server. For Tableau Cloud there is a hosted version, so you paste an address and sign in as yourself.

  1. Ask your Tableau admin whether MCP is allowed on your site. (Tableau's page does not spell out admin steps, so confirm locally.)
  2. In Claude: Customize → Connectors → + Add → Add custom connector. Name: "Tableau". URL: https://mcp.tableau.com.
  3. Sign in with your Tableau account. Sign-in uses OAuth 2.1, so Claude sees your permissions only.
  4. Test with the kinds of questions Tableau's own page shows: "Which are the most viewed workbooks?" or "Show me the top 5 states by sales."
Using Tableau Server?

Your IT team runs a self-hosted copy (it signs in with a Personal Access Token). That is not a self-service setup for most learners.

Be honest with learners

We read Tableau's official repo but have not tested this exact click path in a live Claude account. test once before class

Walkthrough 6 · ChatGPT and Gemini

Same idea, different doors

ChatGPT
  1. Ask your admin to turn on developer mode (Business/Enterprise/Edu).
  2. Plugins page → + → Add custom MCP server.
  3. Paste the app's remote MCP address (it must end in /mcp) and sign in.
  4. Review the tools it found, then test with a read-only prompt.

Slack's docs do not list ChatGPT as a client. Notion and Tableau URLs are public; whether each works in ChatGPT test it.

Gemini
  1. Check Google's conditions: US, 18+, personal account, English, Keep Activity on.
  2. gemini.google.com → Settings → Connected Apps → Add a custom app.
  3. Paste the MCP address, follow the sign-in.
  4. In a chat type @ and pick the app.

Google Docs, Gmail and Calendar are already inside Gemini's own apps. No MCP needed for those.

The skill you learn is the same everywhere: find the app's remote MCP address, add it, sign in, test read-only. Only the menu names change.

Steps summarized from OpenAI developers and Gemini Apps Help · official

Your first test

A four-step test ladder (copy it)

Run these one at a time, in order. Stop at the first step that fails. Replace the [brackets].

Step 1 (who am I?): Which account are you connected to in [app name]? Tell me the name or email you see, and nothing else.

Step 2 (look, don't touch): List the 5 most recent items you can see in [app name]. Titles only.

Step 3 (read one): Open the item called [practice item] and summarize it in 3 bullets. Say which tool you used.

Step 4 (only if 1 to 3 worked): Do not change anything. Tell me which actions you are able to take in [app name] that could change or send something.

Step 4 shows you what the connector is allowed to do, before you ever ask it to do it.

Troubleshooting · 2 of 2

"Which account is it using?"

The most common surprise: Claude is connected, but to your other account (personal vs work). Or you cannot see a file you expected.

  1. Ask it. Use step 1 from the test ladder: "Which account are you connected to in [app]? Tell me the name or email."
  2. Check the file's sharing. Claude can only open files your connected account can open. If you cannot open it in your browser while signed in as that account, neither can Claude.
  3. Fix the account. In Customize → Connectors disconnect or remove the connector. Sign out of the wrong account in your browser, then connect again and choose the right one on the sign-in page.
  4. Revoke old access. You can also remove Claude's access from the app's own security or connected-apps settings.

Reassuring fact: Claude inherits your permissions and its restrictions only narrow access. It never grants more than the app already allows you.

Use case 1 · Drive + Docs

"Find that doc and tell me what it says"

Illustrative workflow, not a real AI transcript.

Before

You remember a proposal from last spring. You search Drive, open four versions, skim each, copy the key terms into a chat to get a summary. 15 minutes.

After

One message. Claude searches Drive, opens the right file, and gives you the terms with the file name so you can check. About 1 minute.

How you do it, day to day

  1. Switch on the Drive and Docs connectors for this chat.
  2. Type: "Search my Drive for the 2026 Acme proposal. Open the latest version and list the price, dates and conditions. Give me the file name."
  3. Open the file it names and check two facts yourself.
  4. Follow up: "Write 3 bullets I can paste into an email to the team."

The habit that matters is step 3: ask for the file name, then spot-check.

Use case 2 · Docs + Slides

From a written report to a first-draft deck

Illustrative workflow, not a real AI transcript.

Before

You have a 6-page Google Doc. You make a blank deck, copy headings in, shorten sentences, fix the layout. An hour.

After

Claude reads the Doc and creates a new Slides deck you edit in the side pane. You spend the hour improving it, not typing it.

  1. Connectors on: Google Drive, Docs and Slides.
  2. Type: "Read [paste Doc link]. Create a 7-slide Google Slides deck for my manager: one idea per slide, a short title and 3 bullets each."
  3. Read the draft in the pane. Ask for changes slide by slide.
  4. Add any charts yourself. Claude can't add charts to Google Slides (Claude Help).

Slides edits may not appear live in the pane. If so, use Open in Google on the file card.

Use case 3 · Gmail + Calendar

Prepare tomorrow in two minutes

Illustrative workflow, not a real AI transcript.

Before

Evening: open the calendar, open each meeting, search email for the last thread with that person, write yourself notes.

After

One request returns tomorrow's meetings, with the last relevant email thread for each, and suggested talking points.

  1. Connectors on: Gmail and Google Calendar.
  2. Type: "List my meetings tomorrow. For each one, find the most recent email thread with the attendees and give me a 2-line reminder of where we left off."
  3. Pick one: "Draft a short email to [name] confirming the agenda. Draft only, do not send."
  4. Read the draft in Gmail, edit, and send it yourself.

Notice the pattern: "draft only, do not send." The human presses send.

Use case 4 · Notion + Slack

Catch up on a project after time away

Illustrative workflow, not a real AI transcript.

Before

Back from leave. You scroll three Slack channels, open five Notion pages and ask a colleague what you missed.

After

Claude searches both places for the project and gives you one catch-up note, with the pages and threads it used so you can dive deeper.

  1. Connectors on: Notion and Slack.
  2. Type: "Catch me up on the Atlas project since 10 October. Search Notion for the project page and Slack for mentions of Atlas. Give me: decisions, open questions, who owns what. Name the page or channel for each point."
  3. Open the two items that matter most and verify.
  4. Optional: "Add a 'Catch-up notes' section to the Notion page." (A write action, so Claude will ask first.)

This mixes private workplace data and a way to write back. Keep the tools narrow and review any write. Chapter 16 explains why.

Use case 5 · Tableau

Explain a dashboard in plain English

Illustrative workflow, not a real AI transcript.

Before

You screenshot the dashboard, paste it into a chat and ask what it means. The AI guesses from a picture and cannot check the numbers.

After

Claude asks Tableau for the view or data, using your permissions and your site's definitions, then explains it in words.

  1. Connect Tableau (walkthrough 5).
  2. Type: "Find the view called Weekly Sales. Tell me what changed versus last week, in 5 bullets for a non-technical reader."
  3. Ask: "Which numbers did you read exactly? List them."
  4. Compare two of those numbers with the dashboard itself.

Tableau's own pitch is that with its MCP server, Claude can use your definitions and respect your security boundaries. That is a vendor claim, so step 4 is still your job.
