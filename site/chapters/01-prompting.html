Chapter 1

How to talk to an AI so it actually helps

The five-part recipe behind every good prompt. No tech background needed.

Press → to begin

The big idea

Think of the AI as a brilliant new colleague

Very smart, very fast, and knows nothing about your company, your audience, or your taste. It only knows what you tell it.

The more precisely you explain what you want, the better the result. Anthropic's prompting guide, which describes the AI as "a brilliant but new employee who lacks context on your norms and workflows"

The colleague test. Show your prompt to a person who knows nothing about the task. If they would be confused, the AI will be too.

Constraints

Length, tone, things to avoid.

Output format

Table? Bullets? Email?

You don't need all five every time. Google's own guide says the same: use a few and it helps. Add more when the answer misses.

Same request, two ways

Weak vs. strong

Weak

"Write an email about the delay."

Strong

I manage a 6-person sales team. Write a short apology email to our biggest client about a 3-day delivery delay. Under 120 words, warm but professional, don't promise a refund. Give me 3 subject lines first, then the email.

Same AI, same minute. The only difference is what you told it.

Parts 1 and 2

Context and task

Context: say why

Explaining the reason helps the AI make better choices on details you didn't mention.

"Keep the tone casual because this is our Friday team newsletter" teaches more than "Be casual."

Task: one clear action

Use a verb: write, summarize, compare, plan, rewrite, list.

Ask for action when you want action. "Rewrite this shorter" gets a result. "Could you maybe suggest changes?" often gets only advice.

Parts 3, 4 and 5

Examples, constraints, format

Examples

Paste a sample you like and say "write the next one in this style."

Start with one. Anthropic suggests 3 to 5 for best results.

Constraints

Word count, tone, audience, must-include points, things to avoid.

Say what to do: "write in paragraphs" beats "don't use bullets."

Output format

A table, 3 bullet points, an email with a subject line, a one-page brief.

If you don't say, the AI picks. Often not what you wanted.

Habits that help

Ten habits to remember

  1. Say exactly what you want.
  2. Explain why.
  3. Be specific: length, audience, limits.
  4. Show an example.
  5. Say what to do, not what to avoid.
  1. Allow "I don't know."
  2. Ask for action when you want action.
  3. Split big jobs into steps.
  4. Iterate. The first answer is a draft.
  5. Keep it simple.

Try this line: "If the information doesn't support a conclusion, say so instead of guessing." It cuts down made-up answers.

Source: Anthropic, "Best practices for prompt engineering" (claude.com/blog)

Real life

Before and after

Office

Before: "Summarize this meeting."

After: "These are notes from a 45-min project meeting. Summarize for my manager who wasn't there: 3 decisions, open questions, action items with owners. Max 150 words."

Marketing

Before: "Write a caption."

After: "3 Instagram captions for our new cold coffee. Audience: college students. Playful, not salesy. Under 25 words, one emoji max."

Student

Before: "Explain photosynthesis."

After: "I'm a first-year biology student with an exam Friday. Explain photosynthesis simply with a kitchen analogy, then quiz me with 5 questions."

Steal this

A template you can reuse

Context: I'm a [role] at [type of company]. This is for [audience] because [reason]. Task: [One clear action: write / summarize / compare / plan / rewrite] Examples: [Paste a sample you like, or "none"] Constraints: [Length, tone, things to avoid, must-include points] Output: [Table / 3 bullets / email with subject line]

Power moves

  • Give a role: "Act as a careful HR manager."
  • Ask it to ask you: "Before you start, ask me up to 5 questions."
  • Label your material: mark where instructions end and the document begins.

Your turn

Mini activity: 10 minutes

  1. Pick a real task from your week (an email, a summary, a plan).
  2. Write the lazy version in one line. Run it.
  3. Rewrite it with the five-part template. Run it again.
  4. Reply to the AI with one correction. Compare all three answers.
Tip: review the output for clarity, relevance and accuracy before you use it. The AI can sound sure and still be wrong.
