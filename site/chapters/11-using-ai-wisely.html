Chapter 11

Using AI wisely

Good prompts and skills get you fast answers. This chapter is about knowing when to trust them.

A framework to remember

The 4 Ds of AI fluency

D1

Delegation

Decide what to give to AI, and how.

D2

Description

Say what you want clearly enough to get something useful.

D3

Discernment

Judge how good the output really is.

D4

Diligence

Own what you do with AI, and how you do it.

The framework is by Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller with Anthropic, and it's the backbone of Anthropic's free "AI Fluency" course. The wording above is our paraphrase of their definitions. D1 · Delegation

What should AI do, and what should you?

The framework describes three ways of working together with AI: automation (AI does the task), augmentation (you and AI work as a team), and agency (AI acts on its own on your behalf, within limits you set).

I do it
Sensitive, personal, or judgment-heavy.
We do it
I draft the idea, AI sharpens; or AI drafts, I edit.
AI does it
Repetitive and low-risk. This is where skills shine.

Mini activity (3 min): list five tasks from your week and sort each into one of the three notes.

D2 ↔ D3

The describe-and-check loop

The course teaches that Description and Discernment feed each other: you describe, you look at the result, you describe again, better.

  1. Describe with the five parts from Chapter 1.
  2. Read the answer as if a new colleague wrote it.
  3. Name what's off in one sentence ("too formal", "the third number is wrong").
  4. Describe again with that fix. Repeat once or twice.

When a fix keeps repeating, put it into a skill (Chapter 5). That's how your own skills get better.

D3 · Discernment

A 60-second check before you use it

  • Facts: are names, dates and claims real? Spot-check the ones that matter.
  • Numbers: recompute one total yourself.
  • Sources: if it cites something, open the original. Google's AI course also teaches checking findings against the originals.
  • Missing: what would an expert say is left out?
  • Tone & fairness: does it sound like you, and is anyone described unfairly?

Helpful prompt: "List the claims in your answer that I should verify, and say how confident you are in each."

AI can sound sure and still be wrong. The more it matters, the more you check.

D4 · Diligence

You are still responsible

  • Protect other people's data. Check your organization's rules before pasting client, customer, student or health information into any AI tool.
  • No passwords or keys in prompts, files, or skills.
  • Skills are stored data. Anthropic's docs say skill definitions and run data follow its standard retention policy (skills aren't covered by zero-data-retention arrangements).
  • Be open about AI help where your workplace or school expects it.
  • You sign off. "The AI wrote it" is not an excuse for an error.

Your turn

Run your own 4D check

  1. Delegate: pick one task and decide if it's "I do", "we do" or "AI does".
  2. Describe: prompt it with the five-part recipe.
  3. Discern: run the 60-second check. Write down one thing you caught or confirmed.
  4. Diligence: note what you did not paste, and who needs to know AI helped.
Share one "I caught it!" moment with the group. It's the best proof that checking works.
