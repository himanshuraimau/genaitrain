Chapter 17 · Use cases

Connectors and plugins, by the job to be done

Claude and ChatGPT now let you link your tools and install ready-made kits. This chapter starts from what you want to get done, then shows seven worked examples you can copy.

Checked on 2 Nov 2026. Every example is labeled illustrative, not a real AI transcript. Tags show how sure we are.

Three words, one picture

Key, recipe, starter kit

Connector (Claude) · App (ChatGPT)

The key

Opens one of your tools so the AI can look inside and, if you allow it, act: Drive, Slack, Notion, Gmail, Tableau.

Skill

The recipe

Written steps for how you like a task done: your report format, your email voice (Chapters 2 and 13).

Plugin

The starter kit

A ready-made bundle of skills and connectors (and sometimes commands or agents) that you add in one go, for a whole job such as sales or support.

Claude's own docs put it this way: connectors give access, skills teach the task, and plugins package skills, connectors, commands and agents into one unit you can add once and share.

Naming: ChatGPT now calls connectors apps, and its plugins can include apps and skills. Menu names differ between accounts and change often.

Claude · plugins

Add a plugin in Claude, step by step

  1. Open Customize → Plugins (claude.ai or the desktop app).
  2. Choose Discover to browse Anthropic's catalog, the directory, and any your organization offers. Open one to read what it contains, then select Add.
  3. Open the plugin's Connectors tab. Each connector shows Connected, Not connected or Not added. Connect the ones you use, one at a time.
  4. Use it: describe the task and Claude loads the matching skill, or type / in the message box and pick plugin-name:skill-name.
Good to know
  • Adding a plugin does not sign you in to anything. You connect each service yourself.
  • Its skills load even without connectors, but a skill cannot reach a service until that connector shows Connected.
  • On Team or Enterprise, an Owner adds the connector for the organization first.
Where it works
  • Skills, commands and connectors: chat, Cowork (tasks in the desktop app) and Claude Code (the coding tool).
  • Agents and hooks do not run in chat, only in Cowork and Claude Code.
  • Turn it off with Disable plugin, or choose Remove.

Worked example 1 · Claude · Sales plugin

Prepare for a customer call

Illustrative workflow, not a real AI transcript.

Before

You open the CRM, search Slack, scroll old notes and paste scraps into a chat. 25 minutes, and you still miss the last thread.

After

One request pulls what your connected tools hold about the account into a one-page brief, with sources, in a couple of minutes.

  1. Add the sales plugin. Connect only the tools you really use (for example Slack and your CRM).
  2. Test read-only: "List the last three conversations about [company]."
  3. Run the prep (below). Read it, then open the two sources that matter most.
Use the sales plugin to prepare me for my call with [company] on [day].
Look in my connected tools for: past conversations, open deals and the latest notes.
Give me: who I am meeting, what they care about, 3 questions to ask, and one risk.
Name the source for every point. Do not send or change anything.

Example 2 · customer-support plugin

Job: a pile of tickets, a few urgent ones. Bundles: Slack, Intercom, HubSpot, Notion and others. Rule: draft replies, never send.

Both prompts say what to produce, what to name (sources) and what not to do (send, change). Reuse that shape for any job.

Five things to ask for

Find, summarize, compare, draft, update

Nearly every connector job is one of five moves. Go in this order: the first three only look, the last two make things.

1 FIND:      Find [the thing] in [app]. Give me the title and link of the best 3 matches.
2 SUMMARIZE: Summarize [that item] in 5 bullets for someone who has not seen it.
3 COMPARE:   Compare [item A] with [item B]. What changed? What do they disagree on?
4 DRAFT:     Draft [an email / a reply / a page] from what you found. Draft only, do not send.
5 UPDATE:    Show me the exact change you want to make in [app]. Wait for my yes before doing it.
Why this order

Looking is low risk. Drafting is reversible. Updating or sending changes the real world, so it comes last, with your approval.

Always add

"Name the source for every point." It lets you check the answer in ten seconds instead of trusting it.

Worked example 4 · for students

Exam prep from your own notes

Illustrative workflow, not a real AI transcript. Works with a Drive connector and a Calendar connector in Claude, or the matching apps in ChatGPT.

Before

Notes in five docs, an exam in three weeks, a calendar full of lectures. You make a plan on paper and drop it by Thursday.

After

The AI reads your notes and your free time, then proposes a plan you can fit around lectures. You approve it before anything is added.

Use my Drive notes in the folder [Biology] and my calendar.
1. List the topics my notes cover, and the topics from [exam syllabus] they do not.
2. Make a study plan from today to [exam date] around my free time. Show it as a table.
3. Add 3 quiz questions per topic from my own notes, with the note each came from.
Do not add anything to my calendar yet. I will say "go" when the plan is right.

After you say "go", the AI asks before it writes to your calendar. If your school account blocks connectors, ask your IT help desk, or try a personal account.

ChatGPT · plugins

Add a plugin in ChatGPT, step by step

  1. Open the Plugins tab in ChatGPT on the web or desktop.
  2. Browse the directory. It has tabs for OpenAI, your workspace and Personal, plus a row of what you have Installed.
  3. Open a plugin's details and select the plus button to install it.
  4. Sign in to any service it asks for. Some ask at install, others at first use.
  5. Use it: describe the result you want, or type @ to choose a plugin or one of its skills.
Plugins listed in the docs
  • Gmail: work with your mail.
  • Google Drive: across Drive, Docs, Sheets and Slides.
  • Slack: summarize channels or draft replies.
Good to know
  • Uninstalling removes the plugin only. Connected services stay connected until you disconnect them.
  • Workspace-installed plugins may not offer Uninstall.
  • Plugins are not in the IDE extension.

For team leads and admins

Give a whole team the same setup

The use case: "Everyone in support should have the same kit, on day one, and nobody should have to hunt for it."

  1. Pilot with a few people. Upload or sync the plugin and share it with 3 to 5 people first.
  2. Add its connectors for the organization. In Claude, an Owner does this under Organization settings → Connectors. Each member then connects with their own account.
  3. Choose the level. In Organization settings → Plugins & skills, set Available to install, Installed by default or Required.
  4. Watch use. The Inventory page shows how many people used each plugin in the last 30 days.
Claude (Enterprise)

You can set a plugin differently for each user group. A group's setting replaces the organization's for its members.

ChatGPT

Admins manage workspace plugins and can sync a GitHub marketplace for the team. Company-managed app accounts are set up under Workspace connections.

Safety

A plugin is someone else's setup. Check it first.

A plugin's skills are instructions Claude follows, and its connectors act with your account. So before you add one:

  • Who made it? Anthropic and OpenAI list plugins in their directories. Others come from a marketplace link or an uploaded file.
  • Was it reviewed? Anthropic scans and reviews directory plugins. Its docs say that review does not cover a plugin you add by URL or upload. Add those only from sources you trust.
  • Which connectors does it bundle? Connect only the ones you need, and start read-only.
  • What runs on your computer? In Cowork and Claude Code, agents, hooks and "Runs in each session" connectors run commands on your machine.
  • Can you undo it? Know where Disable plugin and Remove are before you need them.

The "lethal trifecta" from Chapter 16 still applies: private data, untrusted content and a way to send out, all in one chat.

Worked example 7 · make your own

Turn your skills into a team plugin

The use case: you built weekly-report and meeting-followup in Chapter 16. Now your whole team wants them. We have not run this in a live account.

  1. Open Customize → Plugins → Add and choose Create with Claude.
  2. Describe what to bundle (prompt below). Claude builds the plugin with you.
  3. Test it on practice data. Check that each connector shows Connected.
  4. On Team or Enterprise, share it with specific people first, then publish it to your organization when it works.
Help me create a plugin called team-weekly.
Include my two skills: weekly-report and meeting-followup (I will paste them).
It should use these connectors: Tableau, Google Slides, Google Calendar, Gmail.
Add one command, /team-weekly:friday, that runs the weekly report and then asks if I want a meeting follow-up draft.
Every skill must be read-only by default and draft, never send. Ask me questions before you build it.
