Chapter 13 · Everyday skills

Save it once. Use it every day.

Six skills for the work you do all the time: slides, meeting notes, email, weekly updates, spreadsheets, social posts.

The pattern

Teach it once, then just ask

Step 1 · once, 5 minutes

Save the skill

You write down how you like it done: your slide style, your email voice, your report format. You save it as a skill.

Step 2 · every day, 10 seconds

Say a few things + the skill name

"Make a deck about last quarter's sales. Key points: ... Use my-deck-style." Everything else comes from the skill.

Without a skill

You re-type 15 lines of style rules for every deck. You forget one. Every deck looks a little different.

With a skill

One line, and every deck follows your rules. Every time. Even your teammates can use it.

Setup that applies to all six

How to save it, how to call it

Save (pick one)

  • Paste and ask: open a chat, type "Create a skill from the text below and save it", paste the skill. (Chapter 10, route A)
  • Upload a file (Claude): folder + SKILL.md + zip, then Customize → Skills. (Chapter 10, route B)

Call it

  • Claude: mention the skill by name, or let it pick the skill from the description.
  • ChatGPT: type @ and pick the skill, or let it match automatically.
  • Gemini: type / and the skill name, or let it match automatically.

Skills stack. For slides, the built-in PowerPoint skill builds the file and your skill says how it should look. Both work together (Gemini describes this as stacking skills in one prompt).

Shortcut

Don't want to write it? Let the AI learn from your best work

Attach your best deck, a few emails you're proud of, or last month's report, then paste this:

Look at what I've attached. These are examples of my best work.
1. Describe my style and rules in plain bullets: look, structure, tone, what I always do and never do.
2. Then create a skill named [my-deck-style] that captures them, with a description that says what it does and when to use it. Keep the description under 200 characters.
3. Ask me up to 5 questions if anything is unclear before you save it.

This follows Anthropic's own advice for building skills: have the AI capture what worked into the skill, then edit it yourself.

Use case 1 · save once

Slides in your style

You make decks every week. They should look and read the same way: your colors, your fonts, one idea per slide, a clear takeaway. Today you explain this to the AI every time.

---
name: my-deck-style
description: Designs presentations in my personal style: colors, fonts, slide structure and tone. Use whenever I ask for a deck, slides, presentation or PowerPoint, even if I don't mention my style.
---
# My deck style

Fill in the [brackets] once, then delete this line.

Look
- Colors: background [off-white #FAF7F0], headings [dark navy #1F2A44], accent [orange #E8743B].
- Fonts: headings [Georgia], body [Calibri]. Body text never smaller than 18 pt.
- One idea per slide. At most 6 lines of text. Prefer a chart or picture to a bullet list.
- Footer: [Company name] and slide number.

Structure
1. Title slide: title, one-line subtitle, [my name], date.
2. Slide 2 is "The point": the one-sentence takeaway.
3. Every body slide title is a full sentence stating the takeaway, not a topic label.
4. Last slide: decisions needed or next steps.
5. Aim for [8] slides unless I say otherwise.

Tone: [plain, confident, no jargon].

Always
- Add 2 to 3 sentences of speaker notes to every slide.
- If the audience or purpose is missing, ask me once, in one message.
- When done, list the 3 slides you are least sure about.

Fill in once: Fill in the [brackets] once: colors, fonts, your name, how many slides you usually want. (Description: 185 characters.)

Use case 1 · use every day

Just say this

Your daily prompt 
Make a deck for my manager about last quarter's sales.
Key points: sales up 12%, churn up in the South region.
Decisions needed: hire one more rep, extend the discount offer.
Use my-deck-style.
What you get (example)
  1. Title: Q3 Sales Review · [my name] · date
  2. The point: Sales grew 12%, but the South region is losing customers.
  3. Sales grew 12% quarter on quarter (chart)
  4. Churn rose sharply in the South (chart)
  5. Two decisions we need from you (hire one rep; extend the discount)
  6. Next steps

Same navy headings, same footer, speaker notes on every slide, plus a note: "Least sure about slide 4: I had no churn numbers, so the chart is a placeholder."

Without the skill you'd re-explain about 15 lines of style rules for every deck. Example outputs are illustrative, not real AI transcripts.

Use case 2 · save once

Meeting notes to action items

After every meeting you need the same thing: what was decided, what's still open, and who does what by when. Your notes are messy every time.

---
name: meeting-actions
description: Turns rough meeting notes into decisions, open questions and an action table with owners and dates. Use when the user pastes meeting notes or asks for a meeting summary or action items.
---
# Meeting actions

Use only what is in the notes. Never invent names, dates or decisions.

1. If I haven't said who will read this, ask: "Who is this for, and how long should it be?"
2. Write three parts: Decisions (3 to 5 at most), Open questions, Action items.
3. Show action items as a table with columns: What | Who | By when.
4. If an owner is missing write "owner?". If a date is missing write "date?".
5. Keep it under 150 words unless I ask for more.
6. End with one line starting "Things I wasn't sure about:" listing anything unclear in the notes.

Fill in once: Nothing to fill in. Optionally change the word limit in step 5. (Description: 185 characters.)

Use case 3 · save once

Email replies in your voice

You answer similar emails all day. You want them to sound like you, with the same sign-off and the same rules (like never promising a refund), without re-explaining it each time.

---
name: email-reply-my-voice
description: Drafts email replies in the user's own voice with two options and a subject line. Use when asked to reply to, draft, or respond to an email.
---
# Email reply in my voice

My style (edit these lines to match you):
- Short sentences. Warm but direct.
- I sign off with "Thanks," and my first name.
- I never write "I hope this email finds you well".

Steps:
1. Read the email and say in one line what the sender wants.
2. Write a subject line.
3. Write two options: A) warm, B) firm and brief. Each under 120 words unless I say otherwise.
4. Never promise a refund, date, discount or anything I did not mention.
5. After the options, list anything I should confirm before sending.

Fill in once: Edit the "My style" lines once. After a few weeks, add a line for any habit of yours the AI keeps missing. (Description: 140 characters.)

Use case 4 · save once

Your weekly update

Every Friday you send the same kind of update. You jot down a few rough lines and spend 20 minutes making them look professional.

---
name: weekly-update
description: Writes my weekly status update in a fixed format from a few rough lines. Use when I say weekly update or status update, or paste what I did this week.
---
# Weekly update

Use only what I tell you. Never add work I did not mention.

Format
- Subject: Weekly update: [my name], week of [date]
- Done (at most 5 bullets, start each with a verb)
- Next week (at most 3 bullets)
- Blockers (write "None" if there are none)
- Help needed (only if I mention something)

Rules
1. Short and factual. Under 150 words.
2. Keep any numbers exactly as I gave them.
3. If I forgot a section, ask once, then leave it out.
4. If I did not give a date, write [date] and remind me to fill it in.

Fill in once: Change the section names or the word limit to match what your team expects. (Description: 150 characters.)

Use case 5 · save once

Messy lists to clean sheets

Expenses, attendance, orders, survey answers. They arrive messy. You need a tidy table and totals, and you can't risk the AI quietly changing a number.

---
name: tidy-sheet
description: Cleans messy lists into a tidy table with totals and flags odd entries without changing amounts. Use when the user pastes expenses, attendance, sales or survey data and wants a clean sheet.
---
# Tidy sheet

Never change an amount, a name or a number. Flag problems instead of fixing them silently.

1. Ask which date format I use if any date is ambiguous (like 3/4).
2. Put the data in a table with clear columns.
3. Flag: duplicates, missing values, ambiguous dates, and anything unusual.
4. Give totals by category and say how they were calculated.
5. Suggest one chart that fits the data.
6. List every issue you flagged at the end, then offer to create an Excel file.

Fill in once: Nothing to fill in. Add your own category names in step 4 if you have a fixed set. (Description: 189 characters.)

Use case 6 · save once

Posts in your brand voice

You write posts and captions for the same brand all the time. The voice, hashtags and length rules are the same every time, but the AI doesn't know them.

---
name: brand-voice-posts
description: Writes social posts and captions in our brand voice with fixed lengths and hashtags. Use when asked for posts, captions, ads or announcements for the brand.
---
# Brand voice posts

Brand: [Brand name]. Audience: [who we talk to].
Voice: [playful, warm, never pushy]. Words we like: [fresh, easy]. Words we avoid: [cheap, guaranteed].

Formats
- Instagram: at most [25] words, one emoji, three hashtags.
- LinkedIn: 80 to 120 words, a hook in the first line, at most three hashtags.

Rules
1. Give three options for each platform I ask for.
2. Never invent offers, dates, prices or health claims. If I did not give one, write [CONFIRM] where it would go.
3. End with one line: "Check before posting:" listing every [CONFIRM] and every claim I should verify.

Fill in once: Fill in your brand name, voice words, audience and length rules once. (Description: 156 characters.)

Keep improving

A skill gets better every week

  • When the AI gets something wrong, fix the skill, not just that one answer. Add one line.
  • Marcus Moretti (Chapter 6) says his daily briefing is "just a Markdown file" and he has adjusted it about 50 times.
  • Test with a real request after each edit. Keep a bad result to check you've fixed it.
Week 1: it nearly works.
Week 3: it sounds like you.

If it doesn't trigger

Mention the skill name in your prompt, then rewrite the description with the words you really use.
